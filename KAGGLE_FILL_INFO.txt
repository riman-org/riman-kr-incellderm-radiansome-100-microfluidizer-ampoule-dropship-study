### Overview

Background: The transition from traditional wholesale to zero-inventory drop-shipping models requires rigorous validation of product efficacy and logistical feasibility. This study evaluates the INCELLDERM Radiansome 100 Microfluidizer Ampoule, focusing on its patented microfluidizer technology and Giant BYoungPool Centella Asiatica complex within the South Korean market. Methods: A stratified survey (N=200) was conducted among independent planners and consumers across Jeju, Busan, and Gangnam. Metrics included Likert-scale sensory evaluations, Net Promoter Score (NPS), and adoption barriers for the No Auto-Ship Planner model. Results: The formulation demonstrated high acceptance for texture (Top-2-Box 76.0%) and patent-driven confidence in Niacinamide stability (Top-2-Box 86.0%). However, regulatory trust remained low (Top-2-Box 14.0%), and 'Lack of Physical Samples' emerged as the primary barrier (95.5%). NPS stood at +1.5, with 79.5% passives indicating a need for stronger engagement. Strategic Implications: Success in the cold-start phase depends on providing clinical data infographics (76.0% demand) and addressing sample logistics. The 'Small Batch' preference (67.5%) suggests a hybrid fulfillment strategy is optimal for mitigating margin compression risks while leveraging the brand's scientific authority.
