\begin{abstract}
Background: The transition from traditional wholesale to zero-inventory drop-shipping models requires rigorous validation of product efficacy and logistical feasibility. This study evaluates the INCELLDERM Radiansome 100 Microfluidizer Ampoule, focusing on its patented microfluidizer technology and Giant BYoungPool Centella Asiatica complex within the South Korean market. Methods: A stratified survey (N=200) was conducted among independent planners and consumers across Jeju, Busan, and Gangnam. Metrics included Likert-scale sensory evaluations, Net Promoter Score (NPS), and adoption barriers for the No Auto-Ship Planner model. Results: The formulation demonstrated high acceptance for texture (Top-2-Box 76.0\%) and patent-driven confidence in Niacinamide stability (Top-2-Box 86.0\%). However, regulatory trust remained low (Top-2-Box 14.0\%), and 'Lack of Physical Samples' emerged as the primary barrier (95.5\%). NPS stood at +1.5, with 79.5\% passives indicating a need for stronger engagement. Strategic Implications: Success in the cold-start phase depends on providing clinical data infographics (76.0\% demand) and addressing sample logistics. The 'Small Batch' preference (67.5\%) suggests a hybrid fulfillment strategy is optimal for mitigating margin compression risks while leveraging the brand's scientific authority.

\vspace{0.4em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} RIMAN Skincare, INCELLDERM, Microfluidizer Technology, Zero-Inventory Drop-Shipping, Korean Dermatology, Centella Asiatica
\end{abstract}

\section{1. Formulation Architecture \& Study Methodology}
The INCELLDERM Radiansome 100 Microfluidizer Ampoule represents a convergence of advanced delivery systems and botanical sourcing. Central to this formulation is the proprietary 'Radiansome 100 Microfluidizer' patent, which utilizes high-pressure homogenization to create uniform lipid nanoparticles. This structure encapsulates active ingredients such as Niacinamide and the Giant BYoungPool Centella Asiatica extract, enhancing transdermal penetration and stability. The methodology employed a mixed-mode survey design targeting N=200 participants in South Korea, stratified by region (Jeju Island, Busan, Gangnam Seoul), skin type, and professional role (B2B planners vs. C-end consumers). The study specifically examined the viability of the 'No Auto-Ship Planner' model, where independent sellers operate without mandatory monthly re-consumption or inventory holding. Data collection focused on sensory attributes (texture, absorption), perceived scientific value, and logistical preferences under a zero-inventory framework.
\section{2. Quantitative Performance \& Microclimate Adaptation}
Quantitative results indicate strong performance in sensory dimensions critical for humid climates. The lightweight, non-sticky texture achieved a mean score of 3.83 with a Top-2-Box percentage of 76.0\%, confirming suitability for the high-humidity environments prevalent in Jeju (68\% RH) and Busan (58\% RH). Absorption speed of the Centella complex scored a mean of 3.76 (Top-2-Box 70.5\%), aligning with Korean consumer preferences for rapid skincare routines. Notably, the 'Microfluidizer' patent significantly boosted consumer confidence in ingredient efficacy; 86.0\% of respondents agreed that the technology enhanced their trust in Niacinamide stability (Mean 4.06). However, microclimate analysis revealed slight variations: while Jeju residents showed robust acceptance (Mean 3.74), Gangnam participants reported lower overall satisfaction (Mean 3.66), potentially due to higher expectations for urban-specific anti-pollution benefits. The Squalane-based hydration system maintained integrity without excess sebum production (Top-2-Box 74.0\%), validating its use in combination and oily skin types.
\section{3. Cross-Demographic, MBTI \& B2B/C-End Stratification}
Demographic stratification reveals distinct adoption patterns. Affluent/High Net Worth individuals exhibited the highest composite mean (4.00), driven by appreciation for patented technology and premium positioning. In contrast, Lower-Middle Income segments scored lowest (3.52), citing price sensitivity and margin concerns. Occupationally, Aesthetic Clinic Owners (B2B) and Healthcare Professionals (C-End) showed higher endorsement rates (Means 3.86 and 3.83 respectively), likely due to their ability to clinically validate the Centella soothing benefits. MBTI analysis indicated that Thinking-Judging types (ENTJ, ESTJ) valued the logical consistency of the 'Descending INCI' list and patent claims, whereas Feeling types prioritized ethical sourcing stories. Regarding the drop-shipping model, 67.5\% preferred 'Small Batch' initial orders (5-10 units), reflecting a risk-averse approach to capital deployment. Key recommendation drivers included Centella Soothing Benefits (93.0\%) and Microfluidizer Patent Efficacy (74.5\%), while major barriers were Lack of Physical Samples (95.5\%) and Margin Compression (82.5\%).
\section{4. Regulatory Compliance \& Regional Expansion Strategy}
Regulatory perception remains a critical vulnerability. Only 14.0\% of respondents expressed full confidence in MFDS compliance regarding labeling and safety data (Mean 3.14), highlighting a trust gap that must be addressed through transparent communication. For regional expansion, the study identifies 'Clinical Data Infographics' (76.0\%) and 'Technical Patent Explainers' (58.0\%) as the most effective marketing assets for cold-start success. To mitigate the 'Lack of Physical Samples' barrier, a hybrid fulfillment strategy is recommended: offering single-unit trial kits via drop-shipping while maintaining small local inventory hubs for key influencers. The Net Promoter Score of +1.5, with 79.5\% passives, suggests that while dissatisfaction is low, active advocacy is limited. Enhancing post-purchase engagement through educational content on the Giant BYoungPool sourcing process can convert passives into promoters, securing long-term retention in the competitive Korean dermo-cosmetic landscape.

\section{Conclusion}
The INCELLDERM Radiansome 100 Microfluidizer Ampoule demonstrates strong product-market fit in South Korea, particularly regarding texture suitability for humid climates and consumer trust in patented delivery technologies. However, the zero-inventory drop-shipping model faces significant hurdles related to sample availability and regulatory transparency. Strategic implementation should prioritize the distribution of clinical data infographics and technical patent explainers to bridge the trust gap. By addressing the 'Lack of Physical Samples' barrier through hybrid fulfillment and enhancing MFDS compliance visibility, RIMAN can elevate the NPS from passive neutrality to active advocacy, ensuring sustainable growth for independent planners in the competitive Korean skincare sector.
